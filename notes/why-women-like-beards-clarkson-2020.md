# Why women like beards and masculine faces (Clarkson et al. 2020)

## Phase 1: Assumptions stripped

Common belief : Women prefer masculine traits because they signal good genes and health.

What this study found : That story is mostly wrong. When 919 women rated male faces, their preferences had almost nothing to do with "pathogen disgust" (fear of disease) or wanting babies.

Common belief : Masculine faces and beards work the same way — both signal "manliness."

What this study found : They are totally different. Women treat them like separate questions. It's like asking "do you want a fast car?" versus "do you want a red car?" — speed and color don't predict each other.

## Phase 2: What actually happened

The study showed women composite faces that varied in:
- Masculinity : Feminized → normal → masculinized
- Beardedness : Clean-shaven vs. full beard
- Context : Short-term vs. long-term relationship

## What they found

Main effects : Bearded faces rated more attractive. Unmanipulated and highly masculinized faces beat the intermediate or feminized ones.

The weird part : Scientists expected women who fear disease to prefer masculine faces (strong immune system = good genes). Instead, that fear predicted nothing about face preference. It DID predict beard preference — but in the opposite direction. Women who feared disease liked beards MORE, not less.

What actually predicted masculinity preference : Moral disgust (being grossed out by ethical violations). Nobody predicted that.

Social learning : Women with bearded fathers and bearded partners preferred beards. Experience matters more than biology.

## The analogy

Imagine you are picking apples at a store. Evolutionary psychology claimed you'd pick the shiniest, biggest apples because they signal "healthy tree." But actually, you pick apples based on:
- Whether your parents always bought shiny apples
- Whether your roommate likes shiny apples
- Some random preference you cannot explain

The "health of the tree" theory barely mattered. That's what happened here.

## Why it matters

This was a big study (919 people) with good methods. Most of what it tested was supposed to work — according to dozens of prior papers. It didn't. This suggests those prior papers might be wrong, or the effects are so small they don't replicate.

**The one-sentence summary:** Women like beards and masculine faces for different reasons, and those reasons have surprisingly little to do with evolutionary stories about health and disease.
