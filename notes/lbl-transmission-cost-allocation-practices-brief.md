# Transmission Cost Allocation Practices

Technical Brief from Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory (May 2025)

Authors: Fritz Kahrl, Josh Schellenberg, Lisa Schwartz (LBNL); Diana Rucavado
Rojas, Brittany Tarufelli, Kyle Wilson, Juliet Homer (PNNL)

Funding: U.S. Department of Energy Grid Deployment Office under LBNL Contract
No. DE-AC02-05CH11231

## Overview

This brief provides an overview of different approaches to allocating costs of
transmission facilities between generators and loads, among different
jurisdictions, between customer classes, and for reliability, economic, and
public policy drivers. It discusses lessons learned from stakeholder engagement
for transmission projects between jurisdictions with differing public policy
goals, using counterfactuals for transmission cost allocation, and Federal
Energy Regulatory Commission (FERC) and court decisions on cost allocation
proposals.

The brief has five main sections:

1. Transmission cost allocation methodologies
2. Transmission cost allocation to generators and specific customer classes
3. Planning approaches and stakeholder processes for public policy projects
4. Considerations related to potential use of counterfactuals for transmission
   cost allocation
5. Controversial and rejected cost allocation proposals

## Key Concepts

### FERC Order 1000 Drivers

FERC Order 1000 defined three kinds of transmission drivers:

Reliability transmission : Needed to meet the North American Electric
Reliability Corporation (NERC) reliability standards.

Economic transmission : Cost-effectively reduces transmission congestion when
the transmission system already meets reliability standards.

Public policy transmission : Needed to meet public policy goals.

These three drivers are often difficult to isolate, complicating transmission
cost allocation.

## Cost Allocation Methodologies

### Generator Interconnection

When generators request interconnection, grid operators conduct system impact
studies to determine whether network upgrades are needed to:

1. Ensure generators can operate reliably
2. Allow generators to deliver energy to loads for resource adequacy
3. Enable participation in capacity markets

In most RTOs/ISOs, network upgrade costs are allocated to generators. ISO-NE,
NYISO, and PJM use a "but for" method, which makes generators responsible for
the cost of network upgrades incurred but for the interconnecting generators.

| RTO/ISO | Cost Allocation Method                                                                |
| ------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| CAISO   | Generators pay initial cost; CAISO refunds costs over time (FERC crediting policy)    |
| ERCOT   | Limited network upgrades; transmission providers pay for incremental costs            |
| ISO-NE  | Generators pay (but for method)                                                       |
| NYISO   | Generators pay (but for method)                                                       |
| MISO    | Generators pay; can be refunded for up to 10% of upgrades of 345 kilovolts and higher |
| PJM     | Generators pay (but for method)                                                       |
| SPP     | Generators pay                                                                        |

Interconnection-related network upgrades account for significant transmission
investment:

PJM : 14% of total cumulative board-approved RTEP projects ($48 billion) as of
December 2023.

MISO : 13% of $27 billion of approved transmission projects (2020-2024).

### Transmission Planning Cost Allocation Methods

#### Load Ratio Share (Postage Stamp)

Allocates transmission costs across a defined geographic area in proportion to
load (energy MWh or peak demand MW).

Advantages : Transparent and easy to implement. Updates can reflect changing
demand patterns. Mitigates free ridership. Incorporates intangible benefits
difficult to quantify.

Disadvantages : Shares may not align with actual use of transmission system.
Sub-regions with high peak demand could have high charges even if load does not
use the system. Misalignment if based on consumption instead of coincident peak
usage.

Used by : CAISO, ERCOT, ISO-NE (all or most projects); MISO MVP and LRTP
projects.

#### Zonal (License Plate)

Assigns transmission costs to the sub-region where infrastructure is located.
Load-serving entities in the zone are responsible for costs.

Characteristics : Most often used for lower voltage or local facilities. Can be
incorporated into hybrid cost allocation approaches.

Disadvantages : Challenges for large regional lines crossing multiple zones.
Potential lack of alignment with sub-regional benefits. Inadequately captures
non-monetary benefits. Increases potential for free ridership.

Used by : MISO, SPP (lower voltage projects within single sub-region).

#### Highway-Byway

Hybrid model allocating costs according to voltage level, combining Load Ratio
Share and Zonal methods. As voltage increases, geographic scope of cost
allocation broadens.

Used by : SPP

#### Granular Approach (Beneficiary Pays)

Based on distribution of benefits.

Used by : Market efficiency (economic) projects in most ISOs/RTOs.

#### Power Flows

Based on share of power flows contributing to a reliability problem or
transmission solution.

Used by : PJM

#### Public Policy Supplement

Reduction in total cost based on supplemental contribution of a specific state,
with remaining cost allocated to other states in proportion to benefits.

Used by : ISO-NE

## FERC Decisions and Controversial Proposals

### MISO MVP Portfolio Challenge (2022)

FERC rejected a challenge to cost allocation for MISO 2011 MVP Portfolio. A
Commissioner noted that no information was presented on the level of independent
scrutiny MISO received on its 2017 benefit-cost ratio analysis.

### PJM RTEP Window 3 Projects (2024)

Maryland People's Council contested costs of 2022 RTEP Window 3 projects,
arguing they should be allocated to Virginia due to state subsidies for data
centers. FERC concluded that none of the projects qualified as Multi-Driver
Projects with a state Public Policy Requirement component.

Commissioner Clements' opinion : Emphasized regional nature of PJM transmission
system. Noted difficulty disentangling effects of public policies on
transmission need. Argued reliability should not suffer if upgrades contingent
on voluntary cost assumption. Warned Maryland would be a free rider if it did
not participate.

Commissioner Christie's opinion : Questioned whether transmission projects
resulting from state policy choices should be considered public policy projects.
Proposed FERC convene states to review definition of public policy transmission
projects.

### PJM-NJBPU State Agreement Approach (2022)

FERC accepted the State Agreement Approach between PJM and New Jersey Board of
Public Utilities, noting that no costs will be allocated to customers outside
New Jersey unless FERC accepts a future cost allocation filing.
